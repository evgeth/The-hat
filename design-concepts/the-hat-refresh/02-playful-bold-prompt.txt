Use case: ui-mockup.
Create a high-fidelity redesign concept for the existing iPhone party word-guessing game "The Hat".
Input images: 1 is the existing home screen; 2 is game setup; 3 is player handoff before a turn; 4 is active gameplay; 5 is post-turn word review. All five are functional/content references, not layouts to reproduce. Preserve this game's identity, mechanics and five-screen flow. This is a local pass-the-phone game. No accounts, online multiplayer, subscriptions, feeds or unrelated new features.
Output: one exceptionally polished, large landscape design board, approximately 3600 x 1800, with EXACTLY FIVE complete, equal-size, upright iPhone screens in ONE horizontal row, generous clear gutters. Each screen has realistic 440:956 proportions, minimal dark edge outline, rounded corners, appropriate safe areas, consistent small status bar and home indicator. Flat straight-on product UI, no perspective, desk, props or hands. All five screens fully visible with readable text. A small quiet board heading "The Hat" and subtitle "02 / Playful bold" above the phones; discreet labels beneath each: "Home", "Set up", "Handoff", "Play", "Review". Devote almost all the board area to the five screens.
Design objective: materially improve hierarchy, usability and typography, while keeping a friendly social game character. Use consistent 24pt margins, deliberate 8pt spacing, 16–20pt body type, clear headings, readable 44pt+ touch targets, restrained 16–24pt corner radii. Important actions near the bottom within thumb reach. No unreadable decorative tiny labels. Use native-feeling crisp iconography; always label unfamiliar icons. Color must never be the only action cue.
Exact contents per screen:
1 HOME. Small language control "English" with globe in the top right. Large beautiful "The Hat" wordmark, modest refreshed green top-hat illustration with two paper word slips. Short copy "Good words. Great company." One dominant bottom-area CTA "New game" with arrow. Smaller, clearly secondary rows "How to play" and "Game history", subtle "About" link. A well-composed balance of illustration, type and intentional breathing room.
2 SETUP. Simple back chevron, heading "New game". Section "Players" and "4 players". Compact list of Alice, Bob, Carol, David with letter avatars, reorder handles, and clearly labeled "Add player". Below the list, a logical compact "Game settings" group with full-width aligned rows: "Words in the hat" with minus, "60", plus; "Turn length" with minus, "30 sec", plus; "Difficulty" with value "Normal" and chevron (a selection affordance replacing the permanent huge picker); "Game mode" with a readable segmented choice "Singles" / "Pairs", Singles selected. Visible primary bottom CTA "Continue". Everything fits naturally, with no large dead middle space and no crowded controls at the bottom edge.
3 HANDOFF. Small top row "Your turn" and "60 words left". Large title "Alice, you're up". Clear supporting text "Pass the phone to Alice". Beautiful central role layout with avatar A and "Alice" plus explicit "Explains", arrow or connection, avatar B and "Bob" plus explicit "Guesses". "30 seconds" as concise timing context. Small instruction "Describe the word without saying it." Dominant bottom CTA "Start turn". Clear, calm handoff state with no word revealed yet.
4 GAMEPLAY. Top row "Alice → Bob" and a small labeled "Pause" control. Clear prominent timer "0:27" with a restrained ring or progress indicator. "60 words left" as smaller context. One huge centered word "lighthouse" on a very subtly elevated or outlined word card, which receives dominant attention. Two large persistent bottom buttons, secondary warm terracotta "Mistake" with X icon and primary green "Got it!" with check icon. Keep these separated and visually distinguishable. Text and icons encode meaning as well as color. No distracting illustration or app navigation here.
5 REVIEW. Header "Review turn", names "Alice → Bob". A concise "6 correct" success summary plus "1 mistake". Compact legible editable word list with exactly these seven words: river, thunder, garden, library, mountain, harbor (each with a check and "Correct"), candle (with X and "Mistake"). Supporting hint "Tap a word to change its result." Single bottom CTA "Save & continue". This is turn review, NOT an invented final winner leaderboard.
Avoid: giant handwritten font for all interface text, diffuse giant shadows, glossy glass pills, excessive cards inside cards, gratuitous gradients, childish mascot faces, neon colors, purple/blue palette, dramatic perspective, presentation marketing filler, device overlap, clipped screens, lorem ipsum. Preserve the original green/cream identity, but confidently rethink composition. Make every screen look like part of one implementable native iPhone application.
Art direction for this alternative: a decisive contemporary graphic party-game identity. Flat rich deep forest-green app backgrounds, warm cream typography and muted pale chartreuse accents sampled from the original green hat, with warm terracotta reserved for mistakes. Modern substantial geometric rounded sans-serif typography throughout; confident large wordmark and generous large gameplay word. No serif typography and no hand-lettering. Restrained thin-line icons. Replace the old illustrated hat with a simple distinctive flat graphic top-hat silhouette and two cream paper word slips, using only green and cream, no character face. Think expertly typeset independent tabletop game, youthful and welcoming to adults. Keep tactile charm through composition and tiny playful marks, not 3D rendering or gradients. All controls absolutely flat: no shadows, glow, glossy or beveled surfaces. Lighter green list surfaces with thin quiet dividers, pale chartreuse primary buttons with deep-green text, clearly readable cream secondary text. The central active word can occupy a large cream card with deep-green lettering as the single contrast focal point. Use fewer boxes and more confident typography and space. Crisp clear functional sans-serif text on every button. Preserve full readability and touch-target sizes. Board background neutral warm ivory. Five screens must feel materially different from the old screenshots but recognizably The Hat.
